Asection 1. The drain (High)A

1. The drain (High)

Wallet 0x78c9b1… (the signing wallet) created the contract 0x6cebdf… in its first transaction, 0x477c8060aa22478b436d6120ffe5954775cb02974acd09d7c52ae1500b73d4d9, at 14:50:11 UTC. At 15:49:59 UTC it called that contract, which created 0xf6d072… inside the transaction and drew the five tokens out of the vesting contract. The exploit contract received 99 % or more of the gross outflow of each token, the rest went to a fee-share recipient. A block builder (Quasar Builder) took a 0.01 ETH tip in that transaction; it is a terminal.

UNCX's incident report describes the mechanism as an accounting flaw in the vesting manager: released shares leave the global total but stay in the position's deposited shares, so a later cancel deducts them again. (Medium, as described in UNCX's incident report.)

Asection 8. Method and confidenceA

8. Method and confidence

High = read from a signed transaction, a contract creation, a Transfer log or a balance call. Medium = an inference from High facts or a press statement. Low = a hypothesis. The files: attacker_addresses.csv (10 addresses: 4 on Ethereum, 4 on BNB Chain, 2 on Robinhood Chain, with a chain column), hops.csv (57 transfers in 46 rows, identical transfers of one transaction folded into one row with a count column; transfers from the attacker addresses, the exploit contract and the funder's 22 Sep recipient, with the receiving side classified as attacker-held, terminal, victim, funder or open lead), drain_txs.csv (14 rows, 10 transactions touching the vesting contract on 13 to 15 Oct, labelled attacker, other actor, ordinary release or inbound deposit).

Terminal rule: routers, pools and a block builder end the trace. A funding wallet is listed as an attacker funder only when it both funded the attacker wallet before the drain and received funds back from it.
